
A dozen makeshift shelters cluster in a hollow just off the road, constructed from salvaged canvas, broken cart boards, and desperation. Cook fires have burned down to embers. The smell of unwashed bodies and fear hangs thick in the air.

At first, no one moves. Then a child's cry cuts through the silence, quickly muffled. Faces appear at tent flaps—hollow-eyed, gaunt. These people have been here for over a week with nowhere to go.

When they realize you're not more refugees—that you're armed, capable, _alive_—something shifts. An old man with Shuriman features struggles to his feet, his weathered hands trembling. A young hunter, one arm bound in bloody cloth following behind.

"Thank the ancestors," someone whispers. Then louder: "They sent someone. They finally sent someone."\
