The carpenter's home is modest but well-built—Henrik's own work, sturdy and true. The door stands ajar. Inside, the signs of hasty flight: a chair knocked over, a child's wooden toy abandoned on the floor.

The workshop out back still smells of sawdust, a ghost of normalcy in this nightmare.

In the bedroom, a jewelry box sits open on the dresser. Most of it is simple stuff—wooden beads, a pressed flower in glass. But tucked in the velvet lining, overlooked in the panic of escape: a necklace. The chain is tarnished silver, but the pendant catches the light—a small disc of hammered gold, etched with radiating lines like sun rays.

Amara's necklace. A Shuriman heirloom passed down from her grandmother.

Henrik fled without it. If the party brings it back to the refugee camp—or later, to him in Quathala—it might be the thing that convinces him to return to Stonewall.
