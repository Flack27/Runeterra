
Carved directly into the mountainside, weathered by centuries of wind and rain, the relief emerges from the rock like something half-remembered.

Figures march across the stone in faded relief—tall warriors in elaborate armor, carrying weapons that seem too large for mortal hands. Above them, a disc—radiating lines like a sunburst, or perhaps an eye, watching from the heavens. The relief has split down the middle, cracked through the great disc like a broken medallion. The warrior figures stand divided—half on the left, half on the right. Between them, where solid stone should be, a passage descends into darkness. The edges of the split are clean, precise, as if the mountain itself simply _decided_ to part.

And the smell.

It rolls up from that darkness like breath from a sick throat. The burnt copper scent is overwhelming here, so thick you can taste it on your tongue—metal and wrongness and that sweetness underneath. Mixed with it now, unmistakable: death. Rot.
