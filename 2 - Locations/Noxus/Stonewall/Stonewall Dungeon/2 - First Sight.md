
You smell it before you see it.

A smell of burnt copper and wet stone, carried on a breeze that should smell of ocean and woodsmoke. Beneath it, something sweeter—cloying, wrong—like fruit rotting in summer heat.

Then the village comes into view.

A purple haze clings to Stonewall like a burial shroud. It doesn't move with the wind. It _pulses_, slowly, rhythmically, as if the village itself is breathing. Strange light flickers within—not firelight, not starlight, but something that makes your eyes ache if you look too long. The color of bruises. The color of veins beneath pale skin.

Most of the buildings of the village are charred husks of what they once were. Some doorframes still stand, hanging open. Some half standing walls.

In the distance, near the docks, something burns. A ship—masts still visible through the smoke—lists against the pier. The flames cast the only natural light in this place of wrong colors.
