
As you pass the bridge the smell grows stronger. Burnt copper and sweet decay, thick enough to taste.

Then you see them.

Bodies. Dozens of them. Miners, farmers, families. Laid out in rows along the path like cordwood waiting to be collected. Some have been here for days—bloated, discolored. Others look almost peaceful, as if they might wake at any moment.

The jellyfish-like creatures drift among them like terrible shepherds, selecting their cargo with awful deliberation. When one chooses a body, it wraps its tentacles around the head and upper torso and lifts—the corpse rising until it hangs vertical, feet dangling just above the ground.

Hanged. They look hanged. A procession of the dead, carried by floating horrors toward whatever waits in the mountain.
