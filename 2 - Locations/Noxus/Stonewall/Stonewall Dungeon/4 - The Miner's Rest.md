The inn's sign still swings on rusted chains—a pickaxe crossed with a tankard. The door hangs open, darkness pooling beyond the threshold.

As you approach, you see it.

A Voidmantle drifts out from the inn's entrance, its translucent purple membrane pulsing with bioluminescence. Wrapped in its tentacles, held upright like a hanged man, is the body of a heavyset man in a stained apron. His arms dangling, feet dragging across the ground as the creature carries him toward the mountain.

The innkeeper. Mira's father.

The creature hasn't noticed you yet. It moves with terrible purpose, focused on its task.

**Initial Choice:**

- Attack the Voidmantle carrying the body (may alert others inside)
- Let it pass and investigate the inn
- Attempt to follow it

**Inside the Inn:**

The common room is a tomb.

Tables lie overturned, chairs scattered like broken bones. Dark stains—blood, long dried—mark the floorboards in streaks and pools. A half-eaten meal still sits on the bar, covered in flies. The smell of death and that copper-sweet wrongness is overwhelming here.

The light is dim. Windows shuttered or broken. Shadows pool in the corners and along the ceiling beams.

_The shadows on the ceiling are moving._

**Creatures:**

- 3 Voidmantles clinging to the ceiling beams (Perception DC 14 to notice before they drop)
- 2 Voidlings hiding behind the bar (emerge Round 1)

**Environmental Elements:**

- Low light (disadvantage on ranged attacks beyond 30 feet without darkvision)
- Chandelier can be dropped (DEX save DC 12, 2d6 bludgeoning, prone)
- Furniture provides half cover

**After Combat:**

Behind the bar, a trapdoor leads to the cellar. It's been barricaded from below.

If the party calls out or knocks, a thin voice responds—trembling, barely audible:

_"Are... are they gone? Please. Please tell me they're gone."_

**Cellar Survivors:**

- **Greta** - elderly woman, 68, former seamstress
- **Tam and Lira** - siblings, ages 8 and 6, the blacksmith's children

They've survived on stored vegetables and collected rainwater. Greta kept the children calm by telling them stories. They haven't seen sunlight in over a week.

_"We heard the screaming. Then the... the sounds. Like clicking. Like something breaking. Then Mira's father—he pushed us down here, told us to be quiet no matter what."_

Greta's voice breaks. _"We heard him die."_
